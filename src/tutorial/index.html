Build your study notebook.

Pick one reading, set of slides or unit. Study it with Gemini Notebook. Your brain goes first. The bot checks your work second.

The rule behind every stepSteps 1 to 10

Learning with Gemini Notebook

AI should support your learning, not replace it.

This routine shows you how. After setup, every step uses one of three moves.

GenerateYou think first, on paper.
CheckThe notebook checks you. Then you check the notebook.
FixYou name the gap in your own words.

TipKeep this page open on your phone and the notebook open on your laptop. Check off each step as you go. Your checkmarks stay in this browser.

Before you start

What you need

  1. 01

    Generate3 min · screen downDone

    Step 1: Brain first

    Not read yet?Skim the headings in your material first. Then close it and start.

    1. Start the timer below if you want one.
    2. Put your phone face down, or turn your laptop away.
    3. On paper, write three things you remember about your topic.
    4. Write one question you still have.

    Optional timer

    3:00

    WhyPulling ideas out of your own head is called retrieval. It beats rereading, and it works even when you get some of it wrong.

  2. 02

    Setup2 minDone

    Step 2: Get your materials

    Pick the option that fits what you're studying. Any of the three works.

    Option A

    Download a file

    Download the reading or slides from wherever your course posts them, for example, Canvas.

    Save it where you can find it. You'll upload it in step 4.

    Option B

    Use a web link

    Is your reading a web page? Copy its link. In step 4, you'll add it as a website source.

    Option C

    Use your own notes

    Typed notes work too. In step 4, you can paste them as text or add them as a Google Drive file.

    OK to upload?Upload only materials you have the right to use: your own notes, materials your instructor provides, open textbooks. If you're unsure, ask your instructor. Never upload other people's work or personal information.

  3. 03

    Setup2 minDone

    Step 3: Open Gemini Notebook

    Open notebook.google.com (opens in a new tab)
    1. Sign in with your EWU Google account: username@ewu.edu.
    2. Don't use a personal Google account. EWU accounts carry data protections that personal accounts don't.

    New nameUsed NotebookLM before? Same tool. Google renamed it Gemini Notebook on July 16, 2026.

    Heads upButton names can shift as Google updates the app. If a screen looks different, look for three things: Add sources, the chat box and Studio.

  4. 04

    Setup3 minDone

    Step 4: Create your notebook

    1. Create a new notebook.
    2. Name it after your course and unit. Swap in your own names:
      Notebook name

      [Course] · [Unit]

    3. Choose Add sources. Add what you got ready in step 2: upload the file, add a website link, add a Google Drive file or paste text.
    4. Check that your material shows up as a source.

    Add sources takes

    • A file, like a PDF
    • A website link
    • A Google Drive file
    • Pasted text

    RuleAdd course materials only. No classmates' information, health details or grades.

    Three places you'll use

    Sources

    What you add. The chat answers only from these.

    Chat

    Ask questions here. Answers end with small numbered citations like [1].

    Studio

    Makes audio and video overviews, mind maps, study guides, flashcards, quizzes, infographics and slide decks.

  5. 05

    Generate5 minDone

    Step 5: Quiz yourself in the chat

    1. Copy this prompt. Swap [topic] for what you're studying. Then send it in the chat box.
      Type this in the chat

      Quiz me on [topic], one question at a time. Wait for my answer before the next question.

      Any subjectPhotosynthesis, supply and demand, the causes of World War I, the chain rule, informed consent.

    2. Answer each question without looking. No peeking at your sources or your notes.
    3. Keep score on your paper: got it, shaky or missed.
    4. Stop after five to eight questions.
    Needs input

    I'll wait for your answer.

    WhyQuizzing yourself beats rereading. The best AI moment is when it waits for you to think.

  6. 06

    Check3 minDone

    Step 6: Check the receipts

    Receipts are the small numbered citations, like [1], at the end of the notebook's chat answers.

    1. Pick one of the notebook's answers from your quiz.
    2. Click the [1] at the end of it. It jumps to the exact sentence in your source.
    3. Read that sentence and the ones around it.
    4. Ask yourself: does the source really say that?
    Chat answer [1]
    →
    Your source

    Illustration, not a screenshot.

    WhyGrounded doesn't mean correct. The notebook can pull a sentence out of context. Clicking the citation is how you check.

  7. 07

    GenerateCheck5 minDone

    Step 7: Explain it back

    1. Look away from the chat and your sources.
    2. Pick one key idea from your topic. Explain it in 3 or 4 sentences in your own words. Use paper or the box below.
    3. Optional. Type here and the prompt below fills itself in. Saved only in this browser.
    4. Copy the prompt. Swap [key idea] for the idea you explained. Wrote on paper? Type your sentences where it says [paste]. Then send it.
      Type this in the chat

      Here's my explanation of [key idea]: [paste]. What did I miss? Cite my sources.

    5. Read what it says you missed. Click a [1] to check it.
    6. Add the missing piece to your paper, in your own words.

    WhyExplaining it yourself shows you what you actually know. The notebook's job is to find your gaps, not to write the explanation.

  8. 08

    GenerateCheck4 minDone

    Step 8: Flashcards from Studio

    1. Open Studio and make flashcards.
    2. Quiz yourself for real. Say or write each answer before you check the card.
    3. Mark the cards you missed on your paper.
    4. Pick one shaky card. Studio outputs don't show citations, so check it in the chat. Paste the card's text after this question:
      Type this in the chat

      Where in my sources does this come from? [paste the card]

    5. Click the [1] in the answer and read the sentence.

    OptionalMake an audio overview in Studio. Replay it later this week as review. It will sound smooth, and smooth isn't the same as knowing. Quiz yourself again after you listen.

    WhyFlashcards only help if you quiz yourself. Just reading them is rereading, and a smooth read feels like knowing when it isn't.

  9. 09

    Check2 minDone

    Step 9: Catch one mistake

    1. Look back at the quiz, the feedback on your explanation and your flashcards.
    2. Find one thing the notebook got wrong, oversimplified or left out.
    3. Write it down in one sentence, in your own words.

    Stuck?Pick one claim and ask the chat, “Where in my sources does this come from?” Click the citation and read the sentence. Then compare it with what your class focused on. The notebook only knows your sources.

    Catch report

    GEMINI NOTEBOOK · ONE ANSWER

    What kind of miss?

    Caught the AI's mistakeWIN

    Read it like an editor, not a fan.

    Caught me? That's a win.

    WhyEven with citations, the notebook can misread a passage, flatten a nuance or skip what your class focused on.

  10. 10

    Fix1 min · in your notesDone

    Step 10: Exit line for your notes

    Write this in your notes. Fill in both blanks.

    Exit line

    AI helped me ___. It didn't help with ___.

    WhyThis one is yours, so don't ask the bot to write it. Naming what AI didn't help with shows you where your own brain still has work to do.

Skip these

Prompts that think for you.

Each one hands your thinking to the bot. You finish faster and learn less.

  • For you“Summarize [this reading] so I don't have to read it.”
  • For you“Write my reflection for me.”
  • For you“Give me the answers to the homework.”

The prompts in steps 5, 7 and 8 work the other way. The notebook asks, waits and checks. You do the thinking.

Five rules for using it well.

  • @

    Use your @ewu.edu account.

    Not a personal login. EWU accounts carry data protections that personal accounts don't.

  • ×

    Upload course materials, not people.

    No classmates' information, health details or grades.

  • i

    Say when you used AI, and how.

    One line is enough. For example: “I used Gemini Notebook to quiz myself on the chain rule.”

  • ✎

    Reflection stays yours.

    Write reflections in your own words.

  • ?

    Every course sets its own AI rules.

    Check the syllabus, and ask before you submit if you're unsure.
