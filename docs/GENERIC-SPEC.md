# Generic wording spec: any course, any discipline

Travis's direction (Oct 6, 2026): every version must work for any teacher in any course. Remove all course-specific content: ITGS 196, First-Year Seminar, Week numbers, Chapter 4, section numbers, OpenStax / College Success, the Canvas Read page path, the course journal, the course's Week 10 ethics unit, the course learning outcome, "degree path", and the "ten weeks" arc. Keep everything about Gemini Notebook, the research, the learning science and the mascots.

Institution: keep EWU. Students sign in with their @ewu.edu account.

## Sources (what students put in a notebook)
- "Your course materials": a reading, lecture slides, a lab manual, a problem set, a case study, an article, your own notes.
- Get them wherever your course posts them (for example, Canvas).
- Upload only materials you have the right to use: your own notes, materials your instructor provides, open textbooks. If you're unsure, ask your instructor. Never upload other people's work or personal information.

## Placeholders and examples
- Prompts use bracketed placeholders: [topic], [key idea], [this reading], [this unit].
- Discipline-spanning examples, used sparingly: photosynthesis, supply and demand, the causes of World War I, the chain rule, informed consent.

## Standard prompts (use these exact sentences)
- Quiz: "Quiz me on [topic], one question at a time. Wait for my answer before the next question."
- Explain: "Here's my explanation of [key idea]: [paste]. What did I miss? Cite my sources."
- Connect: "Ask me three questions that connect [topic] to my major or my life."
- Where: "Where in my sources does this come from?"
- Mind map: "Make a mind map of [this reading]."
- Attend: "List three questions [this reading] raises but doesn't answer."
- Assess: "Make a 10-question quiz on [this unit]. After I finish, show which sources my misses came from."
- Thinks for you: "Summarize [this reading] so I don't have to read it." / "Write my reflection for me." / "Give me the answers to the homework."
- Depends: "Make me flashcards for [this unit]." (great if you quiz yourself, useless if you only read them) / "Which idea in [this unit] do students usually misunderstand?" (useful as a pointer, you still have to test it)

## Exit line
"AI helped me ___. It didn't help with ___." Write it in your notes.

## Five rules
1. Use your @ewu.edu account.
2. Upload course materials, not people. No classmates' information, health details or grades.
3. Say when you used AI, and how. One line is enough.
4. Reflection stays yours. Write reflections in your own words.
5. Every course sets its own AI rules. Check the syllabus, and ask before you submit if you're unsure.

## Across your term (replaces "Your ten weeks")
- Start of term · Try it. Low stakes. One Studio tool a week.
- Early · Learn the method. Do the Notebook Sprint once, on purpose.
- Middle · You choose. Pick the tool that fits the task, and know why.
- End of term · Look back. What helped, what didn't, before finals.

## Other wording
- "This fall, AI got a face." becomes "AI got a face." Mascot dates on the cards stay.
- Saundra McGuire: "Saundra McGuire's Study Cycle" with no event or date.
- Title-slide label: "Study smarter · For any course".
- Sprint: "pick one reading, set of slides or unit" in place of Ch. 4. Step 2 Build: "New notebook. Add your reading, slides or notes."
- Receipts: "Where in my sources does this come from?" Receipt header: "GEMINI NOTEBOOK · ONE ANSWER".
- Writing rules: plain, second person, short sentences, active voice, no em dashes, no emoji. Keep all research numbers and dates exactly as they are.
