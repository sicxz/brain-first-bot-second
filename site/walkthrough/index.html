02/13Hook

Cute is a design decision.

A friendly face lowers your guard. That's the point.

Mascots like these exist to make you trust the AI. That's great for the companies. It can be bad for your learning.

People may treat it “less like software and more like a companion.”

Justin Joseph, quoted in Fortune, October 2, 2026

When AI feels like a friend, you stop checking it. And a friend will happily do your homework for you.

Q: So who is doing the thinking?

Meet Gemini Notebook.

NotebookLMRenamed July 16, 2026

If you've heard of NotebookLM, this is the same tool with a new name. Here's what makes it different from a regular chatbot. You hand it sources, like a reading or slides from your course, and it answers from those sources.

Grounded

It answers from the sources you add.

[1] [2]

Ask in the chat box. Each answer ends with numbered citations. Click one to jump to the exact sentence in your source.

05/13Evidence

Same tool. Opposite results.

Two studies looked at students learning with AI. Same technology. Opposite results.

Study A · about 1,000 high school students practicing math
Practicing with AIOn the test, no AI

Used a regular AI chatbot (like ChatGPT) to get answers

48%better17%worse

Used an AI tutor that gave hints, not answers

127%betterNo dropheld steady

Compared with students who never used AI. Bastani et al., PNAS, 2025.

Study B · 194 Harvard physics students

2×

They learned more than twice as much with an AI tutor that made them do the thinking, in less time than a regular class.

Kestin et al., Scientific Reports, 2025.

The chatbot group in Study A let the bot do the thinking. Once the AI was gone, they did worse than students who never used it.

The tool doesn't decide. How you use it decides.

How learning actually works.

Saundra McGuire's Study Cycle has five steps.

Preview→Attend→Review→Study→Assess↺ repeat

Two ideas sit underneath it.

07/13ApplyYour new rule

Brain first, bot second.

This is the one rule to leave with. Generate, then check, then fix.

  1. 1

    Generate.

    Write what you remember. Try the question first.

  2. 2

    Check.

    Ask the notebook. Click the citation.

  3. 3

    Fix.

    Name the gap in your own words.

The Codex pet helps explain it. Its status bubble has four states: Running, Needs input, Ready and Blocked. For learning, the state you want is Needs input. The AI should spend most of its time waiting for you to think. If the bot never waits, you never think.

Needs input

Needs input: your turn to think. This is the state you want.

The best AI moment is when it waits for you.

  1. Stage 1 of 5

    Preview

    Play the audio overview on the walk to class. Skim the mind map.

    Make a mind map of [this reading].

    A trailer gives new ideas somewhere to land. It starts the cycle. It never replaces the reading.

  2. Stage 2 of 5

    Attend

    Bring one question the notebook couldn’t answer.

    List three questions [this reading] raises but doesn’t answer.

    You arrive with a reason to listen and something to ask.

  3. Stage 3 of 5

    Review

    Right after class, quiz yourself on that day’s topic.

    Quiz me on [topic], one question at a time. Wait for my answer before the next question.

    McGuire’s cycle puts review as soon after class as possible. Retrieval does the work.

  4. Stage 4 of 5

    Study

    Flashcards first. Then explain a concept in your own words and ask what’s missing.

    Here’s my explanation of [key idea]: [paste]. What did I miss? Cite my sources.

    Explaining it yourself shows you what you actually know.

  5. Stage 5 of 5

    Assess

    Take a practice quiz closed-book, then check citations on the ones you missed.

    Make a 10-question quiz on [this unit]. After I finish, show which sources my misses came from.

    Knowing what you know, and what you don’t, is metacognition.

Summaries live at the start of the cycle. Never at the end.

09/13WorkshopWith or for?

Does the prompt think with you or for you?

You don't need to be a prompt expert. You need a few good sentences. Read each prompt and decide: with you, or for you? Then tap the card to check.

With youFor youDepends on how you use it

“With you” prompts put you to work: the notebook asks, waits and critiques. “For you” prompts hand the work to the bot. The strongest move: write your own explanation first, then ask what you missed, with a citation.
